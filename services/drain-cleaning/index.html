A drain that clogs once is a clog. A drain that clogs every few months is a symptom — a belly in the line, root intrusion, a failing section of cast iron, or a fixture that was never vented properly. Clearing it without diagnosing it just resets the clock.

We clear the blockage first, because you need your house back. Then we tell you what we saw and whether it is going to happen again.

What the pattern tells us

  1. One fixture is slow. Usually a local clog in the trap or branch line. Straightforward, and rarely a sign of anything bigger.
  2. Every drain on one side of the house is slow. The problem has moved to a shared branch line. Still localized, but bigger than a trap.
  3. Toilets gurgle when the washer drains. Classic venting or main line restriction. The system is pulling air through the nearest water seal because it cannot get it from the vent.
  4. The lowest drain in the house backs up first. Almost always a main line stoppage. Stop running water and call — this is the one that floods basements.
  5. It comes back every spring. Strongly suggests root intrusion at a joint. Seasonal timing is the tell.

Cast iron, clay and roots

The older neighborhoods of Abingdon, Bristol and Marion have a lot of original cast iron drain and clay sewer line still in service. Both fail in characteristic ways. Cast iron scales inward until the effective diameter is a fraction of what it started as, then cracks at the hub. Clay separates at the joints, and every separated joint is an open invitation to tree roots looking for water.

Roots are the reason a lot of homes here have a “spring clog.” The line is fine in the dry months and closes up when the trees wake up. Cutting them clears it; repairing the joint is what actually ends it.

Septic systems

Plenty of properties in rural Washington, Smyth, Wythe and Grayson counties are on septic rather than municipal sewer, and a backup there can be a plumbing problem or a tank problem. The distinction matters because they have completely different fixes. We will diagnose which side of the tank the trouble is on before anyone starts digging.

Common questions

Are chemical drain cleaners a bad idea?

For a main line, yes. They rarely clear a real stoppage, they sit in the line, and they make the job hazardous for whoever opens it next. On older cast iron they can also accelerate damage. A plunger and then a phone call is the better sequence.

Do you camera the line?

When the pattern suggests something structural — repeat clogs, suspected roots, or an older line — seeing it is worth far more than guessing at it. Call and describe the history and we will tell you whether it is warranted.

Everything in the house is backing up. What do I do right now?

Stop running water immediately, including the washing machine and dishwasher, and do not flush. That is a main line stoppage, and every gallon you add has to go somewhere. Then call us.

My water bill jumped but I do not see a leak.

Often an underground service line leak between the meter and the house, or a toilet flapper leaking silently. Shut every fixture off and watch the meter — if it still moves, you have a leak on the supply side.
