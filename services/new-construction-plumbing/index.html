Rough-in is where a building’s plumbing is actually decided. Once the drywall is up, every shortcut becomes permanent and every correction becomes expensive. We would rather spend the extra hour on layout than have you spend it on access panels later.

We work on new builds, additions, and the kind of projects that are new construction in everything but name — basement bars, added bathrooms, converted spaces that need supply and drain where none has ever run.

How we approach a rough-in

  1. Layout before anything is cut. Drain slope, vent routing and fixture spacing planned against the actual framing, not against an assumption about it.
  2. Supply sized for the whole fixture count. Including whatever is realistically getting added in phase two. Under-sizing the trunk is the most common regret.
  3. Inspection-ready, then pressure tested. Work that passes because it is right, and proven before it is covered.

What we take on

Below-grade and freeze considerations

Two things come up constantly on new work in this region. The first is below-grade drainage: a basement bathroom or bar frequently sits under the level of the sewer main, which means an ejector pump and a properly vented basin rather than a gravity tie-in. Building that in at rough-in is straightforward; retrofitting it is not.

The second is freeze protection. Between the elevation and the crawlspace-heavy building style here, any line running through an unconditioned space needs to be routed and insulated with January in mind. We would rather over-plan that at the framing stage than answer an emergency call about it two winters from now.

Common questions

Do you work with builders and general contractors?

Yes. Call with the project and the timeline and we will tell you honestly whether we can hit your schedule.

Do you do commercial work?

Yes — we handle commercial plumbing repairs and installations alongside residential.

Can you plumb a basement bathroom below the sewer line?

Yes, with a sewage ejector system and proper venting. It is a normal part of a basement build; it just has to be designed in rather than added on.

Do you pull permits?

Permitting varies by jurisdiction across Washington, Smyth and Wythe counties and the City of Bristol. We will tell you what your specific project needs before work starts.
